# Regulate — Product Principles

## 1. Regulation without effort
The experience should bias users toward down-regulation without requiring conscious participation after voluntary initiation.

"Against their will" is an internal provocation meaning the experience should not require conscious participation after the user voluntarily starts it. It is not a product claim that Regulate can override autonomy or guarantee a physiological outcome.

## 2. Passive rather than guided
No explicit inhale, exhale, hold, or breath-counting instructions. The breathing circle functions as an ambient pulse that a person may consciously or unconsciously follow. It should not look like an exercise the user is failing if they do not match it.

## 3. Predictability without obvious mechanical repetition
The ambient rhythm is stable and predictable. Subtlety comes from non-round pace values, layered visual motion, slow fades, organic particle behavior, and different modulation depths across systems — not from randomized timing or LFO jitter.

## 4. One shared rhythm across sensory systems
All audio and visual modulation derives from a single rhythm source. Audio LFOs, visual breathing, particle modulation, and aurora pulsing share one clock. The default is 5.7 cycles per minute.

## 5. Soundscape is separate from mechanism
Calm, Ground, and Drift are soundscapes — tonal palettes — not separate regulatory interventions. The regulatory mechanism (slow, shared rhythm) is the same across all three.

## 6. Calm transitions instead of abrupt changes
All state changes use gradual fades. Crossfades between soundscapes take approximately four seconds. Session wind-downs take forty seconds. Nothing pops in or cuts out.

## 7. User autonomy and easy stopping
The experience is always user-initiated. Stopping is always available and takes effect within seconds. The five-minute reset completes on its own. No guilt, urgency, or loss aversion should discourage stopping.

## 8. Accessibility is part of regulation
A dysregulated user may have limited motor control, low frustration tolerance, or be using assistive technology. The interface must be operable by keyboard, screen reader, and with reduced motion. Inaccessible design undermines the product's purpose.

## 9. User-facing language must not overstate evidence
Scientific and design rationales are preserved in internal documentation. User-facing copy uses general wellness language: "ambient reset," "settle without instructions," "slow audiovisual environment." No claims about nervous system activation, brainwave states, or guaranteed physiological outcomes.

## 10. Future measurement should validate the mechanism
Future research may include HRV, heart rate, electrodermal activity, respiration, and subjective measures. These are research possibilities, not current product capabilities or proof of efficacy. Measurement is deferred to a future phase.
