# Regulate — Future Validation Plan

## Purpose

This document outlines potential future studies to evaluate whether Regulate produces measurable physiological or subjective effects. These are research possibilities, not current product capabilities or proof of efficacy.

## Measure 1: Heart Rate Variability (HRV)

**What**: Time-domain (RMSSD, SDNN) and frequency-domain (LF, HF, LF/HF ratio) HRV metrics.

**Why**: Slow-paced breathing near resonance frequency (~6 cpm) is associated with increased HRV in published literature. HRV increase would suggest parasympathetic activation.

**Method**: Wearable PPG sensor (e.g., Polar H10 chest strap for accuracy) recording continuously during a 10-minute session. Compare 5-minute pre-session baseline to session and 5-minute post-session recovery.

**Expected direction**: Increase in RMSSD and HF power during and after session.

**Limitations**: HRV is influenced by many factors (caffeine, hydration, time of day, fitness). Requires controlled conditions and sufficient sample size (n > 20).

## Measure 2: Heart Rate

**What**: Average heart rate and heart rate trend over session.

**Why**: Relaxation is generally associated with reduced heart rate.

**Method**: Same PPG sensor as HRV. Track minute-by-minute average.

**Expected direction**: Modest decrease (2-5 bpm) over 10-minute session.

## Measure 3: Respiration Rate and Pattern

**What**: Breath rate (cycles per minute) and regularity.

**Why**: The core hypothesis is that passive exposure to 5.7 cpm rhythm encourages unconscious respiratory entrainment.

**Method**: Respiratory inductance plethysmography (RIP) belt or nasal thermistor. Compare spontaneous breathing rate before, during, and after session.

**Expected direction**: Breathing rate moves toward the app's rhythm (5.7 cpm) during session. Key question: does this happen without conscious effort?

## Measure 4: Electrodermal Activity (EDA)

**What**: Skin conductance level (SCL) and skin conductance responses (SCRs).

**Why**: EDA reflects sympathetic nervous system arousal. Decreased SCL and fewer spontaneous SCRs suggest reduced arousal.

**Method**: EDA sensor on non-dominant hand. Continuous recording.

**Expected direction**: Decrease in SCL and SCR frequency over session.

## Measure 5: Subjective Activation Scales

**What**: Self-reported arousal and valence.

**Why**: Physiological measures don't always correspond to subjective experience. Both matter.

**Method**: Brief validated scales (e.g., Activation-Deactivation Adjective Check List, or Visual Analogue Scale for relaxation) administered pre and post session.

**Expected direction**: Decreased activation, increased pleasant affect.

## Study Design Considerations

- **Control condition**: Same visual display without rhythmic modulation, or a nature-sounds app
- **Blinding**: Participants cannot be blinded to condition (they can see/hear the difference), but raters analyzing physiological data can be
- **Sample size**: Power analysis needed; preliminary estimate n=25-30 per group for medium effect size
- **Exclusions**: Cardiac conditions, respiratory conditions, pregnancy, current psychiatric crisis (per onboarding safety information)
- **Ethics**: Institutional review board approval required for any physiological measurement study

## Timeline

These studies are appropriate after:
1. Core experience is stable and user-tested
2. Brand and positioning are finalized
3. Research partnerships are established
4. Funding for equipment and participant compensation is secured

## Important Disclaimer

Until these studies are conducted and results are analyzed, no claims about physiological effects should be made in any user-facing material. The app is a general wellness tool, not a medical device.
